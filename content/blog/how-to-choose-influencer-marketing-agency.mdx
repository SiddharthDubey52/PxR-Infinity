To choose an influencer marketing agency, check four things: how they pick creators (audience fit and engagement, not follower counts), whether they show you a named shortlist with costs, how approvals and reporting work, and whether they’ve run campaigns like yours. A good agency is open about all four before you pay.

This guide gives you a checklist, questions to ask and red flags. For how we work, see our page as an [influencer marketing agency in India](/services/influencer-marketing).

## What an influencer marketing agency should do

- **Find the right creators** for your audience, not just the famous ones
- **Negotiate rates, deliverables and usage rights**
- **Write briefs** that keep your message clear but let creators sound like themselves
- **Manage approvals** so nothing goes live without your OK
- **Schedule posts** so they build momentum
- **Report** views, reach, engagement and clicks, creator by creator

## Influencer agency checklist

| Check | Good sign | Warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator selection | Picks by audience, engagement and content style | Only talks about follower counts |
| Shortlist | Named creators with costs before you commit | “We’ll handle it, trust us” |
| Audience checks | Looks at comments, audience location and past results | No checks mentioned |
| Approvals | You approve every draft | Posts go live without review |
| Reporting | Link to every post, numbers per creator | Totals only |
| Pricing | Clear split of creator fees and agency fee | One number, no breakdown |
| Experience | Campaigns in your category | No relevant examples |
| Contracts | Usage rights and deadlines in writing | Verbal promises |

## Questions to ask before you hire

1. How do you choose creators for a brand like mine?
2. Can I see a sample shortlist with costs?
3. How do you check that a creator’s audience is real and relevant?
4. What do you do if a creator misses a deadline or posts something off-brief?
5. Who owns the content, and can we reuse it in ads?
6. What does your report include?
7. Can you show me a campaign similar to mine?

## Red flags

- **Promises of viral results.** Nobody can promise that.
- **No named creators until after payment.**
- **Only mega creators suggested**, whatever your budget.
- **No usage rights discussion.** You may not be allowed to reuse the content.
- **Reports without links.**

## Matching the agency to your goal

| Goal | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Launch awareness | Agencies that mix creators with meme pages and media |
| Sales or installs | Agencies that track links and codes per creator |
| Film or music release | Agencies with release-week experience and reel audio campaigns |
| Regional audience | Agencies with creators in that language |
| Long-term brand building | Agencies that plan repeat creators, not one-off posts |

## Agency vs doing it yourself

| | Doing it yourself | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Creator fees only | Creator fees plus management |
| Time | Many chats, follow-ups, approvals | One point of contact |
| Rates | Depends on your network | Often better through relationships |
| Tracking | Manual | One report |
| Best for | One or two creators | Many creators, tight timelines |

## How to compare two agency quotes

Put them side by side and check: number and names of creators, formats, posting dates, usage rights, approval steps, reporting, and the split between creator fees and agency fees. The cheaper quote is often cheaper because it leaves something out. For typical price drivers, read [influencer marketing rates in India](/blog/influencer-marketing-rates-india).

## What a good influencer brief looks like

The brief is where many campaigns go wrong. A good agency writes one that is clear about the message but leaves room for the creator’s style:

- **The goal**: what the campaign should achieve
- **Key message**: one or two points, not ten
- **Must-haves**: product shown, link or code, disclosure
- **No-gos**: claims to avoid, competitor mentions, sensitive topics
- **Format and length**: reel, Short, story, video
- **Timeline**: draft date, approval date, posting date
- **Usage rights**: whether you can reuse the content in ads

Ask to see a sample brief before you hire. It tells you a lot about how the agency thinks.

## How to judge results after the campaign

Don’t stop at total views. Look at:

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Views and reach per creator | Who actually reached people |
| Engagement rate | Whether people cared |
| Comments | What people really thought |
| Link clicks or code uses | Who drove action |
| Cost per result | Which creators were worth it |

Keep the creators who performed and drop the rest. Over two or three campaigns you build a reliable group.

## Disclosure

Paid partnerships should be disclosed, using the platform’s paid partnership label or a clear mention. A good agency handles this by default. It protects you, the creator and the audience’s trust.
