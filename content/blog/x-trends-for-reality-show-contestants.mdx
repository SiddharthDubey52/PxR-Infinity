Inside the house, a contestant can’t post, reply or thank anyone. Their supporters outside can, and a lot of that happens on X. **X trends for reality show contestants** keep a contestant visible every week, give fans a shared moment and show media that people are watching.

This guide lays out a week-by-week plan based on the seasons our team has worked on. For the general mechanics of a trend, see [how to plan an X trend campaign](/blog/how-to-plan-an-x-trend-campaign).

## Why trends matter during a season

A season moves fast. Every episode creates new talking points, and the conversation outside shapes how viewers see each contestant. Trends help in three ways:

- **Visibility:** the contestant’s name shows up on the list, even for people who missed the episode.
- **Unity:** fan clubs post together instead of in twenty different directions.
- **Signal:** media pages and portals notice which contestants have an active fan base.

## The core idea: one message a week

If we had to pick one thing from our campaigns, it’s this: choose **one clear message per week** and build that week’s trends around it. When different fan clubs push different lines, they cancel each other out.

For Mridul Tiwari in Bigg Boss 19, that meant a new message each week, fan clubs briefed to post together, and moments such as his captaincy turned into trends of their own. Three of those trends reached #1 in India, including BIGGBOSS USE MRIDUL with 187.9K+ posts. Read the [Mridul Tiwari case study](/work/mridul-tiwari-bigg-boss-19-x-trends).

## A week-by-week trend calendar

| Week | Focus | Trend idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry week | Introduce the contestant | A welcome trend with their name and a one-line identity |
| Weeks 2–4 | Build the story | Trends around their strengths: honesty, humour, strategy, family values |
| Nomination weeks | Get votes | A clear voting reminder trend inside the voting window |
| Big moments | Celebrate | Fast trends for a captaincy, a task win or a family visit |
| Mid-season | Defend calmly | When a clip goes against them, a calm, positive trend with context |
| Finale week | Push hard | Daily trends with one finale message |

## Timing trends around episodes and voting

- **After the episode airs** is when fans are most active and the moment is fresh.
- **Inside the voting window** is when reminder trends matter most. Read [how Bigg Boss voting works](/blog/how-does-bigg-boss-voting-work) for how voting windows usually run.
- **Avoid clashes** with big cricket matches or major releases if you can.

## Organising fan clubs for trends

Trends need many real people posting in the same window. That means fan clubs have to be organised before the season starts:

- One coordinator per club, in one shared group with the PR team.
- A posting time that everyone knows.
- A content bank for each trend: lines, edits, clips and images.
- Clear rules: no abuse, no attacks on other contestants or their families.

Our [fan club guide for contestants](/blog/fan-club-guide-for-contestants) covers roles, rules and a weekly plan in detail.

## Example: one nomination week, day by day

This is roughly how a nomination week goes when the contestant is in danger:

1. **Nomination episode night:** a short post on the contestant’s account explaining the nomination calmly, and a voting reminder pinned on every fan club page.
2. **Day 2:** clips of the contestant’s best moments that week, shared by meme and media pages on Instagram.
3. **Day 3:** the main trend of the week, built on that week’s message, in the evening after the episode.
4. **Day 4:** a live X Space with fan club leads to keep supporters going and answer questions about voting.
5. **Final voting day:** a reminder trend inside the voting window, with simple instructions on how to vote.
6. **Eviction episode:** a thank-you post either way, so supporters feel their effort mattered.

The exact plan changes with each show’s format and voting rules, but the rhythm of message, clips, trend, Space and reminder works across seasons.

## What to avoid

- **Negative trends** against other contestants. They invite backlash.
- **Fake accounts.** X filters them, and it damages the contestant’s image.
- **Too many hashtags in one day.** One focused push beats three weak ones.
- **Promising a position.** Nobody can promise a spot on the list.

## Beyond X

Trends are strongest when the same message also appears on Instagram: meme pages, reality TV media pages and fan edits. That way the contestant reaches viewers who never open X.
