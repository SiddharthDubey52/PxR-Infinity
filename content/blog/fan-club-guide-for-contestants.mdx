Every reality show contestant with a real following ends up with fan clubs: pages, groups and accounts set up by supporters. Left alone, they each do their own thing. Organised, they become one of the strongest assets a contestant has. This **fan club guide for contestants** is a practical handbook for the team running a contestant’s PR and for the club leads themselves.

If you want the bigger picture of how fan armies grow during a season, read [Bigg Boss PR: how contestants build fan armies](/blog/bigg-boss-pr-how-contestants-build-fan-armies). This guide is about running them day to day.

## Why organisation matters more than size

The most common problem we see isn’t too few fans. It’s fans pulling in different directions: one club pushes a hashtag, another starts a different one, a third gets into a fight with another fandom. The result is noise.

When Rajat Dalal entered Bigg Boss 18, his supporters were spread across many separate fan clubs, each saying something different. Bringing them together around one message, shared hashtags, a daily posting rhythm and live X Spaces changed that. Read the [Rajat Dalal case study](/work/rajat-dalal-bigg-boss-18-pr-campaign).

## Set up the structure

### Roles

| Role | Who | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinator | Someone from the contestant’s team | Sets the weekly message, approves content, talks to the family |
| Club leads | One per fan club | Passes the plan to their members, reports back |
| Content makers | Editors and designers among the fans | Makes edits, images and clips for the content bank |
| Spaces host | A confident speaker | Runs live X Spaces and keeps them positive |

### One shared group

Put the coordinator and every club lead in one group. Use it only for plans, content and updates. Keep personal chat elsewhere.

## Write the rules down

Share a short list of rules with every club and pin it:

1. **Stay positive.** Post about our contestant, not against anyone else.
2. **No abuse.** No insults to other contestants, their families or their fans.
3. **No fake accounts.** Real people only.
4. **Use the official hashtag,** spelled exactly.
5. **Don’t speak for the family.** Only the official accounts make statements.
6. **When in doubt, ask the coordinator.**

These rules protect the contestant. A single abusive post from a fan page can become a news story about the contestant.

## The weekly plan

A simple rhythm that works across seasons:

- **Monday:** the coordinator shares the week’s message and hashtag plan.
- **Daily, after the episode:** clubs post clips and reactions within a set time.
- **Mid-week:** a live X Space to keep everyone together.
- **Trend day:** one planned trend around the week’s message. See [X trends for reality show contestants](/blog/x-trends-for-reality-show-contestants).
- **Voting window:** clear, repeated voting reminders.
- **Weekend:** a thank-you post and a quick review of what worked.

## The content bank

Fan clubs post more, and better, when they have material ready. Keep a shared folder with:

- Clips from each episode (as allowed by the platform and the show).
- Edits and images made by the content team.
- Short lines to post in English, Hindi and the regional language of the fan base.
- Voting instructions as a simple image.

## X Spaces

Live Spaces are where fan clubs feel like one community. Keep them focused: recap the week, explain the plan, celebrate wins and remind people to vote. For Rajat Dalal, the “BB TROPHY HAKDAAR RAJAT” Space on 7 January 2025 ran 2 hours 18 minutes with 77K people tuned in.

## Handling a bad week

Every contestant has one. When a clip goes against them:

- The coordinator shares one calm response line with context.
- Clubs post the contestant’s positive moments instead of arguing.
- Nobody targets another contestant or their family.
- The family or team decides if an official statement is needed.

## The finale push

In the last week, everything builds towards one message, usually that the contestant deserves the trophy. Plan daily trends, a big Space before the finale, voting reminders on every platform and a thank-you post ready for whatever the result is.

## After the show

Don’t let the clubs disappear. Thank them publicly, keep them in the loop on the contestant’s next projects and invite them to support future appearances. A loyal fan base is the start of a career after the show.
