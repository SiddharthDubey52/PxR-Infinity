Online reputation management (ORM) means managing what people find and say about a person or brand online, mostly in Google results, reviews and social media, so the picture is accurate, fair and up to date. It covers watching what’s being said, fixing what’s wrong, responding well, and publishing real content that deserves to be found.

In practice, ORM is how you make sure the first page of Google tells the full, current story about you. It’s the long-term side of what a [crisis PR agency in India](/services/crisis-pr-reputation-management) does.

## What is ORM, in simple words?

Think of it this way: before people hire, buy, book or follow, they search. ORM is the work of making sure what they find is true and helps them decide. It’s not about hiding things; it’s about making sure accurate, recent information is easy to find.

## What ORM includes

| Part | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Monitoring | Tracking new mentions, articles, reviews and posts |
| Audit | Checking what Google, YouTube and social show for your name |
| Corrections | Asking publishers to fix facts that are wrong or out of date |
| Platform reporting | Using official tools for content that breaks platform rules |
| Accurate content | Interviews, articles and profiles about your real work |
| Reviews and replies | Responding calmly and helpfully to feedback |
| Profile upkeep | Keeping your own websites and social profiles current |

## What ORM can and can’t do

| ORM can | ORM can’t |
| --- | --- |
| Correct wrong facts through the publisher | Delete true reporting because you don’t like it |
| Report content that breaks platform rules | Promise any article will be removed |
| Help accurate content rank | Promise a fixed position in Google |
| Improve how you respond to criticism | Stop people from having opinions |

If anyone promises to “remove all negative news”, be careful. Honest ORM works through corrections, platform rules and better content.

## ORM for people vs brands

**For people** (celebrities, founders, doctors, creators): the focus is the name search, social profiles, interviews and how fans or followers talk about you.

**For brands:** the focus is reviews, news coverage, social comments and how complaints are handled.

## How ORM works, step by step

1. **Search yourself.** Private window, Google and YouTube, first two pages.
2. **List the problems.** Wrong facts, old stories, missing information.
3. **Correct what’s wrong.** Contact publishers with proof.
4. **Fill the gaps.** Publish accurate, useful content: interviews, articles, updated profiles.
5. **Respond well.** Calm, factual replies to criticism.
6. **Monitor.** Set alerts for your name.
7. **Review every month.** Search results change slowly.

## ORM checklist

- [ ] Google your name or brand in a private window
- [ ] Check image and video results
- [ ] List anything factually wrong, with proof
- [ ] Update your own profiles and website
- [ ] Plan one or two accurate articles or interviews
- [ ] Reply to recent reviews and comments
- [ ] Set up name alerts

## ORM for small businesses

For a shop, clinic, restaurant or service business, ORM is mostly about reviews and search. Reply to every review, good or bad, calmly and helpfully. Fix real problems people mention. Keep your business listing, hours and contact details correct. Ask happy customers to leave a review, but never offer rewards for positive ones or write fake ones. Over time, honest replies and good service do most of the work.

## ORM for public figures

For celebrities, creators and contestants, reputation moves fast on social media. A clip or comment can spread in hours. ORM here means watching what’s being said, responding once and clearly when needed, briefing fan communities to stay respectful, and keeping accurate interviews and coverage coming so search results show the full story. ## Common ORM myths

- **“ORM is just deleting bad news.”** Most of the work is correcting, responding and publishing accurate content.
- **“Ignoring criticism makes it go away.”** Sometimes; often it doesn’t. A calm, factual reply can help.
- **“More positive posts fix everything.”** Volume without substance doesn’t rank or convince anyone.
- **“ORM is a one-time job.”** It’s ongoing, because new content keeps appearing.

## What to do in the first 24 hours of a problem

1. Find out exactly what was said and where.
2. Don’t reply in anger.
3. Decide if a response is needed, and agree one clear message.
4. Correct factual errors with proof.
5. Brief your team and supporters.
6. Start monitoring closely.

## How long ORM takes

Usually months, not days. Search engines update slowly, and new content takes time to rank. It’s ongoing upkeep, like maintaining a house.

## ORM in one example

Say a founder’s name brings up an old, inaccurate article about a business that closed years ago. Honest ORM would ask the publisher to update the facts with proof, publish recent interviews about the founder’s current work, refresh their LinkedIn and company pages, and keep monitoring. The old article may stay online, but over the following months people searching the name find the current, accurate story first.

## Key takeaways

- ORM means managing what people find and say about you online so it’s accurate and current.
- It includes monitoring, audits, corrections, platform reporting, accurate content and replies.
- Honest ORM never promises removal of true reporting.
- Crisis PR handles the first days; ORM handles the months after.
- Small businesses should focus on reviews, replies and correct listings.
- It’s ongoing work, because new content keeps appearing.
