Once a contestant walks into a reality show house, they lose their phone, their voice online and their ability to explain themselves. What happens outside for the next few weeks depends a lot on what was set up before they went in. That’s why **PR before entering a reality show** is worth doing properly. Contestants who do well outside usually had things ready on day one.

This checklist is what our team works through with contestants and families before entry. It applies to Bigg Boss, Rise and Fall, Lock Upp and most other formats.

## 1. Sort out who runs the accounts

Decide, in writing:

- **Who has access** to the contestant’s Instagram, X, YouTube and Facebook. Keep it to one person or one small team.
- **What they will post:** episode reactions, family messages, voting reminders, thank-you notes.
- **What they will never post:** fights with other contestants, replies to trolls, anything about legal or personal matters.
- **Two-factor login and recovery details,** so nobody gets locked out mid-season.

Read your show’s contract rules on social media use before you decide. Some shows limit what can be posted while a contestant is inside.

## 2. Clean up the profiles

Viewers will search for the contestant the moment they appear on screen. Make the first impression count:

- An updated profile photo and bio that explains who they are in one line.
- Pinned posts that show their best side: work, family, personality.
- A review of old posts and comments. Archive anything that could be clipped out of context.
- Consistent names and handles across platforms, so fans find the right accounts.

## 3. Write the story in three lines

Every contestant needs a short, honest answer to “who is this person?” Write three lines:

1. **Where they come from.** Their town, background or journey.
2. **What they’re known for.** Their work, talent or personality.
3. **Why viewers should support them.** The quality fans will see in the house.

Niharika Tiwari’s campaign in Rise and Fall, for example, was built around her journey from Gidam in Bastar to national reality TV. Read the [Niharika Tiwari case study](/work/niharika-tiwari-rise-and-fall-finalist).

## 4. Build a photo and video bank

The outside team will need content every day, and they can’t ask the contestant for it. Collect:

- Childhood and family photos (with the family’s permission).
- Work photos and videos: performances, shoots, projects.
- Short videos of the contestant talking directly to camera: a hello message, a “please support me” message, a thank-you message.
- Photos with friends and co-stars who may post in support.

## 5. Line up fan pages and fan clubs

Supporters start organising the moment the show starts. Get ahead of it:

- Identify existing fan pages and the most active supporters.
- Set up one shared group with fan club coordinators.
- Agree on one main hashtag and a posting time.

Our [fan club guide for contestants](/blog/fan-club-guide-for-contestants) covers roles, rules and a weekly plan.

## 6. Plan day one

The premiere episode is the biggest discovery moment. Prepare:

- **An entry post** on the contestant’s own accounts, scheduled for the premiere.
- **A welcome trend** with their name, timed to the episode. See [X trends for reality show contestants](/blog/x-trends-for-reality-show-contestants).
- **Media and meme pages** ready to share their introduction.
- **A short statement** from the family or team thanking viewers.

## 7. Prepare for the hard moments

Every season has clips that go against someone. Decide in advance:

- Who approves a response, and how fast.
- The calm, factual tone every response will use.
- That nobody on the team attacks other contestants or their families.

Our [crisis PR guide for Indian celebrities](/blog/crisis-pr-guide-for-indian-celebrities) explains how to respond without making things worse.

## 8. Know the voting rules

Before entry, understand how the show’s voting works: which app or platform, when windows open and close, and how many votes each viewer gets. Prepare simple voting instructions that fan clubs can share. Read [how Bigg Boss voting works](/blog/how-does-bigg-boss-voting-work) for an example.
