A single good article can do what months of social posts can’t: it tells people that someone independent thinks your story matters. That’s why brands, founders, artists and public figures all want press. This guide explains **how to get media coverage in India** — step by step, from finding your story to making coverage work after it goes live.

## Step 1: Find the story, not just the news

“We launched a product” is news to you. To an editor, it’s one of hundreds of emails that day. A story is something readers care about. Ask:

- **What’s new or surprising?** A first, a number, a change in how something works.
- **Who is affected?** Real people, a community, an industry.
- **Why now?** Does it connect to a season, a trend, a festival, a release or a news topic?
- **Is there a human angle?** A founder’s journey, a small-town artist, a turning point.

If you can’t explain your story in two sentences, keep working on it before you pitch.

## Step 2: Choose the right outlets

Media in India is huge and varied. Coverage works best when it reaches your audience, not just the biggest name:

| Your goal | Outlets that usually fit |
| --- | --- |
| Investor and customer trust | Business and startup portals |
| Entertainment and celebrity news | Entertainment portals, paparazzi pages, reality TV pages |
| Local customers | City and regional-language news |
| Young audiences | Digital-first media, meme and entertainment pages |
| Niche expertise | Trade and industry publications |

Make a short list, then read what each outlet has published recently. You’ll quickly see what kind of stories they cover — and which journalist covers your topic.

## Step 3: Write a pitch editors actually read

Your pitch should be short enough to read on a phone:

1. **A clear subject line** — the story in under ten words.
2. **Two or three lines** explaining the story and why it matters to their readers now.
3. **What you can offer** — an interview, data, photos, video, an exclusive.
4. **Contact details** and the press release attached or linked.

Personalise every pitch. Mention a recent article by that journalist if it connects. Never send a mass email with every journalist in CC.

## Step 4: Prepare a press kit

When a journalist says yes, they need material fast:

- A clear press release with the key facts
- High-quality photos and logos
- A short bio or company description
- Quotes from the right person
- Links to past coverage, if any

Having this ready makes it easy to say yes to you.

## Step 5: Time it right

Timing can make or break coverage:

- **Avoid big news days** when your story will be ignored.
- **Pitch early** for planned moments like launches and releases.
- **Use moments** — a festival, a season, a trending topic — when your story genuinely connects.
- **Follow up once**, politely, after a few days.

## Step 6: Earned and sponsored coverage

Earned coverage comes from pitching; sponsored coverage is a paid feature. Both have their place. Sponsored features are predictable and quick; earned coverage carries more independent credibility. Many campaigns mix the two — just be clear about which is which.

## Step 7: Make coverage work after it’s live

An article is the start, not the finish:

- Share it on your social channels and website.
- Add it to your pitch deck, profile and media kit.
- Use it to start conversations with partners and investors.
- Let meme pages, creators and paparazzi pages extend the story to audiences who don’t read news sites.

This is where **digital PR** comes in — read [what is digital PR](/blog/what-is-digital-pr).

## Common mistakes

- Pitching an announcement with no story.
- Sending long, generic emails.
- Expecting a top national title on day one.
- Not having photos or spokespeople ready.
- Forgetting to share coverage once it’s live.

If you need coverage across many outlets at once, have a tight deadline, or don’t have time to pitch and follow up, an agency helps. ## Regional media is an opportunity

Many brands only chase national English titles. But regional-language news and entertainment portals have huge, loyal audiences and are often more open to new stories. If your customers are in a particular state, a feature in their language can do more than a national mention.
