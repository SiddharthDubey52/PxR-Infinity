When a journalist wants to feature an actor, or a brand wants to check if an actor fits a campaign, the first thing they ask for is the media kit. If it takes you a day to put one together, the story has often moved on. This **media kit checklist for actors** covers what to include, how to keep it ready and how it gets used.

## What a media kit is for

A media kit (also called a press kit) is a short, ready-to-send package about the actor. It is used by:

- **Journalists and portals** writing a feature, interview or announcement.
- **Brands and agencies** deciding on a collaboration.
- **Event organisers** introducing the actor on stage or in a press note.
- **Casting and production teams** looking for a quick overview.

Ideally they get everything they need without having to ask you a single follow-up question.

## The checklist

### 1. A short bio, in three lengths

| Length | Use |
| --- | --- |
| One line | Captions, event introductions |
| One paragraph (60–80 words) | Articles and press notes |
| Full bio (250–300 words) | Features and interviews |

Write in the third person. Include where the actor is from, their training or start, their best-known work and what they are doing now.

### 2. Photos

- Three to five recent, high-resolution headshots.
- Two or three full-length photos.
- A few on-set or behind-the-scenes photos.
- Photographer credits for each, where required.

Name the files clearly, for example `actor-name-headshot-2026.jpg`.

### 3. Showreel and clips

A showreel of one to three minutes, with the best work first. Add links to two or three standout scenes. Use links that don’t expire.

### 4. Credits

A clean list of films, series, TV shows, ads and theatre work: title, role, platform or channel, and year. Most recent first.

### 5. Press coverage

Links to the best interviews and features, with outlet name and date. Five strong links are better than twenty weak ones. Our guide on [how to get media coverage in India](/blog/how-to-get-media-coverage-in-india) explains how to earn these.

### 6. Social media

Handles for each active platform with the follower count and the date you checked. Add a line on what the audience engages with most: behind-the-scenes, dance, comedy, fitness.

### 7. Talking points

Three to five topics the actor is happy to speak about: their journey, a cause, their craft, an upcoming project. This helps journalists pitch better questions.

### 8. Upcoming work

Upcoming releases with dates, if they can be shared. Mark anything under embargo clearly.

### 9. Contacts

One media contact and one business contact, with phone or WhatsApp and email. Make it clear who handles what.

## A sample one-page layout

If the actor is just starting out, a single page can do the job. This layout fits on one A4 page:

1. **Top:** name, one-line bio, one strong headshot.
2. **Left column:** the one-paragraph bio and three talking points.
3. **Right column:** the five most important credits and one showreel link (or QR code).
4. **Middle strip:** two or three press logos or headlines, with links.
5. **Bottom:** social handles with follower counts and the date checked, plus media and business contacts.

As the career grows, this one page becomes the cover of a longer kit, and the extra pages hold the full credits, more photos and more coverage.

## Keep it ready

- **One shared folder** with the PDF, photos and showreel links.
- **Version dates** on the PDF, so everyone knows which one is current.
- **A WhatsApp-friendly version** under 5 MB.
- **An update reminder** every two or three months, and after every release.

## Common mistakes

- **Outdated photos.** If the actor looks different now, journalists notice.
- **Too long.** Nobody reads a 20-page kit. Keep the PDF to four pages.
- **Broken links.** Check every link before sending.
- **Inflated numbers.** Brands check. Real, dated numbers build trust.
- **No contact details.** It happens more often than you’d think.

## How a PR team uses the kit

A PR team sends the kit with every pitch, shares it with event organisers and paparazzi before appearances, and uses the talking points to set up interviews that fit the actor’s story. It is also the base for [digital PR](/blog/what-is-digital-pr): portal features, interviews and announcements.
