Ten years ago, PR meant press releases, newspapers and TV. Today, most people discover brands and public figures on their phones — through news apps, Instagram, X, YouTube, meme pages and group chats. **Digital PR** is how you earn attention in those places. This guide explains what digital PR is, how it differs from traditional PR, and what a good campaign looks like in India.

## What is digital PR?

Digital PR is the work of getting **other people** to talk about you online — journalists, websites, creators, pages and communities — so that your story spreads with independent credibility.

It includes:

- **Online media coverage** on news, business and entertainment portals.
- **Creator and influencer content** that features your story.
- **Social media pages**, including meme pages and paparazzi pages.
- **Conversations on X**, including trends and Spaces.
- **Search presence** — what people find when they Google you.

The common thread is that someone else is telling your story, not just your own ads or posts.

## Digital PR vs traditional PR

| | Traditional PR | Digital PR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main channels | Print, TV, radio | News portals, social media, creators, search |
| Speed | Days to weeks | Hours to days |
| Measurement | Clippings, estimated readership | Links, reach, engagement, search results |
| Shelf life | Often one news cycle | Stays searchable and shareable |
| Format | Articles, interviews | Articles plus reels, memes, threads, Spaces |

They aren’t opposites. A strong campaign uses traditional media for credibility and digital channels for reach and speed.

## What a digital PR campaign looks like in India

India’s internet is mobile-first, multilingual and fast. A typical campaign combines:

1. **A story angle** that works for both editors and social audiences.
2. **Media placements** on relevant portals, including regional-language sites when it fits.
3. **Paparazzi and entertainment pages** for public figures and releases.
4. **Meme pages** to make the story shareable for younger audiences — read [meme marketing examples from India](/blog/meme-marketing-examples-india).
5. **Creators** who explain or react to the story in their own voice.
6. **An X moment** — a trend or Space — when a big announcement lands.
7. **A report** with every link, screenshot and number.

## Why digital PR matters now

- **Trust:** people believe what others say about you more than what you say about yourself.
- **Search:** when someone Googles your name, recent, credible coverage makes a strong first impression.
- **Speed:** online stories can be live and spreading within hours.
- **AI search:** tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews draw on what credible sites publish about you. Accurate coverage helps them describe you correctly.

## How to measure digital PR

Avoid vague promises of “reach”. Ask for:

- **Every live link and screenshot**
- **Where each piece appeared** and who that audience is
- **Engagement** on social posts — views, shares, saves, comments
- **Search changes** — what shows up for your name or brand
- **Business signals** — enquiries, sign-ups, followers, streams, depending on your goal

## Common mistakes

- Treating digital PR as just “posting more on Instagram”.
- Chasing big reach numbers from pages whose audience doesn’t match yours.
- Ignoring search, so coverage is hard to find later.
- Using fake engagement — it damages trust when people notice.

## Is digital PR right for you?

If your customers, fans or investors form opinions online — and almost all of them do — then yes. Start with one clear goal and a short campaign, measure it, then build from there. ## An example of a digital PR plan

Here’s how a simple four-week digital PR plan for a product launch might look:

| Week | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Story angle, press release, media list, creator shortlist |
| Week 2 | Teasers on social, early creator posts, pitches to portals |
| Week 3 (launch) | Articles go live, meme page posts, creators, an X moment |
| Week 4 | Share coverage, founder interviews, report and next steps |

The same structure works for a film release, a celebrity announcement or a reality-show entry — only the channels and the tone change.

## How long before you see results?

Some results are immediate: articles, posts and trends go live within days. Others build over time — search results improve, people start recognising the name, and enquiries increase. That’s why many brands run digital PR as a monthly plan after their first campaign rather than a one-off.
